\begin{abstract}
We present two results in a Lean 4 development built from the differential theta kernel of the Riemann xi function. First, we exclude equality between the spectral product of a theta--Hankel adjoint square and normalized xi under every constant complex rescaling. A certified Rayleigh estimate forces spectral concentration incompatible with the target's fourth-order Taylor invariant. Second, we certify the strict moment inequality $M_0M_4<3M_2^2$ and factor the degree-two, shift-zero moment-based Jensen polynomial $J^{2,0}$ only into two distinct real linear factors. A partition into 1600 rational cells, explicit exponential bounds, and analytic tail estimates establishes the quantitative gap $3M_2^2-M_0M_4\ge4287/10^8$. The complexified polynomial has exactly the same two real roots. The reported final axiom inspections list only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The spectral exclusion concerns a specific function identity; the Jensen result concerns one polynomial. Neither result proves the Riemann hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Lean 4; formal verification; theta kernel; Hankel operator; spectral product; Riemann xi function; Jensen polynomials; certified integration; Hilbert--P\'olya approach.

\section{Introduction}
A spectral approach to the Riemann hypothesis requires a precise connection between an operator and the nontrivial zeros of the zeta function. Constructing a compact or self-adjoint operator from theta data is only one part of that task. The proposed connection must also satisfy the analytic identities demanded by the target function. This paper examines equality between an even spectral product of an adjoint-square operator and the normalized critical-line xi function, allowing an arbitrary constant complex scale.

The Hilbert--P\'olya viewpoint motivates the search for a suitable spectral realization; Berry and Keating~\cite{berry} discuss the connection with eigenvalue asymptotics. It does not prescribe the operator studied here, nor require every candidate to admit this product representation. Other spectral frameworks, including the trace-formula setting of Connes~\cite{connes}, have different objects and identification problems. Our exclusion theorem tests a concrete candidate, rather than establishing an impossibility theorem for spectral approaches to the Riemann hypothesis.

The obstruction is most naturally interpreted through $Q/E^2$. After normalizing the spectral weights to sum to one, this ratio is their square sum. A sufficiently concentrated spectrum makes the fourth derivative of the product too small relative to the square of its second derivative. Matching the second Taylor coefficient fixes the square of the scale; matching the fourth then imposes a scale-independent constraint. The separation is proved with exact rational inequalities, rather than inferred from floating-point approximations. Lean~4~\cite{lean} and mathlib~\cite{mathlib} supply the formal environment for the analytic, Hilbert-space, spectral, and arithmetic arguments.

The contribution has three parts. First, we connect a normalized $L^2$ test vector to a triangular theta integral. Second, we obtain a lower bound on $Q$ through the spectral basis of $S$, without requiring a separate theorem that the operator norm is attained by an eigenvalue. Third, we combine certified bounds with the second and fourth derivative identities and reduce a hypothetical complex match to an excluded real match. A second contribution formalizes a certified quadratic Jensen inequality, explicit distinct real roots, and a complete root classification after complexification. The mathematical proofs are followed by the formal theorem correspondence and audit procedure.

\subsection{Related work}
The analytic setting belongs to the classical xi-function theory described by Titchmarsh~\cite{titchmarsh}; spectral interpretations are discussed by Berry and Keating~\cite{berry} and Connes~\cite{connes}. The Tur\'an inequalities and Jensen polynomial results of Csordas, Norfolk, and Varga~\cite{cnv} and Griffin, Ono, Rolen, and Zagier~\cite{gorz} provide the context for the quadratic result. Farmer~\cite{farmer} distinguishes Hermite-type asymptotic behavior from evidence sufficient to establish RH, reinforcing the need to state exactly which polynomial family is proved hyperbolic.

A recent Lean companion development by G Six~\cite{gsix} studies a Bost--Connes modular-generator candidate. Its repository explicitly describes a conditional spectral reduction, with additional named axioms and an unconstructed spectral-data hypothesis. We cite its stated scope, without claiming an independent audit of that formalization. It treats a different operator and identification problem from the constant-scale theta--Hankel product tested here. The present paper claims the specified certificates and theorem chain, rather than a general priority claim over all spectral reductions.

\section{The theta kernel and the operator}
\subsection{Normalization and moment conventions}
Let $\theta(t)=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp(-\pi n^2t)$ for $t>0$. Define the logarithmic theta profile $B$ and differential kernel $\Phi$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile}
 B(u)=e^{u/2}\bigl(\theta(e^{2u})-1\bigr),\qquad
 \Phi(u)=B''(u)-\tfrac14 B(u).
\end{equation}
The individual Gaussian profile with parameter $a$ is $2\exp(u/2-ae^{2u})$. Applying $d^2/du^2-1/4$ and setting $a=\pi n^2$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 \Phi(u)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
 \left(8\pi^2 n^4 e^{9u/2}-12\pi n^2 e^{5u/2}\right)e^{-\pi n^2e^{2u}}.
\end{equation}
These factors fix the normalization throughout the paper; no constant is absorbed into $\Phi$ when passing to the operator. For $u\geq0$, each summand is positive: it has the form $2e^{u/2-b}(4b^2-6b)$, where $b=\pi n^2e^{2u}>3/2$. Continuity and weighted decay estimates provide the integrability used below.

Write $\Xi$ for the critical-line xi function as represented in the development. Its cosine representation, valid for complex $z$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi}
 \Xi(z)=\int_0^{\infty}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du,\qquad X(z)=\frac{\Xi(z)}{\Xi(0)}.
\end{equation}
The analytic background for xi is described in Titchmarsh~\cite{titchmarsh}. Here $X$ is the normalized target \code{hpThetaNormalizedXi}; equation~\eqref{eq:xi} makes the kernel and target normalization explicit. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 M_k=\int_0^{\infty}u^k\Phi(u)\,du\qquad(k=0,2,4).
\end{equation}
The positivity certificate gives $M_0>0$, so $X$ is well-defined and $X(0)=1$. Differentiating under the integral, justified by weighted decay, gives $\Xi''(0)=-M_2$ and $\Xi^{(4)}(0)=M_4$. Odd derivatives at zero vanish by evenness. Analytically, strict positivity of $\Phi$ on $(0,\infty)$ also implies $M_2>0$; the denominator-free formal obstruction below needs only the supplied bound $M_2\geq0$.

\subsection{The Hankel operator and its adjoint square}
Work on $H=L^2((0,\infty),\C,du)$, with inner product conjugate-linear in the first argument. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
 K(x,y)=\Phi(x+y),\qquad (Af)(x)=\int_0^{\infty}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy.
\end{equation}
The formula for $Af$ is interpreted almost everywhere. Elements of $L^2$ are equivalence classes; pointwise equality of representatives is neither required nor used. The construction gives a bounded operator with a kernel representation compatible with these classes.

A change of variables on the positive triangle gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \int_0^{\infty}\!\int_0^{\infty}|K(x,y)|^2\,dy\,dx
 =\int_0^{\infty}u\Phi(u)^2\,du=:E.
\end{equation}
Consequently $A$ is Hilbert--Schmidt and compact. The kernel is real and symmetric. These properties do not imply positivity of $A$; the positive operator used here is $S=A^*A$, for which $\pair{f}{Sf}=\norm{Af}^2$. Denote its nonnegative eigenvalues, with multiplicity, by $\lambda_i$. A full orthonormal spectral basis includes the zero eigenspace when present. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sums}
 E=\sum_i\lambda_i,\qquad Q=\sum_i\lambda_i^2,\qquad 0\leq Q\leq E^2.
\end{equation}
The equality between the integral in~\eqref{eq:energy} and the spectral sum is part of the operator-to-spectrum development. Summability and nonnegativity imply $Q\leq E^2$. Zero eigenvalues contribute neither to the sums nor to the product.

\section{The spectral product and its Taylor data}
Define the even spectral product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 P(z)=\prod_i(1-\lambda_i z^2),\qquad z\in\C.
\end{equation}
Since $\sum_i\lambda_i=E<\infty$, finite products over finite subsets converge locally uniformly on $\C$. On $|z|\leq R$, deviations of the factors from one are bounded by the summable family $\lambda_iR^2$. The limit is entire, even, and equals one at zero. Its formal name is \code{hpThetaHankelSpectralProduct}. Although~\eqref{eq:product} has the usual determinant interpretation for a trace-class adjoint square, the proof uses the spectral product directly and does not require a separately formalized Fredholm determinant API.

For a finite index set $F$, expansion of $P_F(z)=\prod_{i\in F}(1-\lambda_i z^2)$ gives quadratic coefficient $-\sum_{i\in F}\lambda_i$ and quartic coefficient $\sum_{i<j}\lambda_i\lambda_j$. Thus
\begin{align}
 P_F''(0)&=-2\sum_{i\in F}\lambda_i,\label{eq:finite2}\\
 P_F^{(4)}(0)&=12\left[\left(\sum_{i\in F}\lambda_i\right)^2-\sum_{i\in F}\lambda_i^2\right].\label{eq:finite4}
\end{align}
The order relation only informally enumerates unordered pairs; the finite-set formula is independent of that ordering. Local uniform convergence allows passage to derivatives, and summability gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivatives}
 P''(0)=-2E,\qquad P^{(4)}(0)=12(E^2-Q).
\end{equation}
The normalized target satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:targetderivatives}
 X''(0)=-\frac{M_2}{M_0},\qquad X^{(4)}(0)=\frac{M_4}{M_0}.
\end{equation}
The formal second derivative uses \code{deriv (deriv f)} and the fourth uses \code{hpThetaIteratedComplexDeriv 4}. The development also identifies its iterated derivative operator with mathlib's \code{iteratedDeriv} in the relevant setting.

For $E>0$, define $p_i=\lambda_i/E$ and $\rho=Q/E^2$. Then $\sum_i p_i=1$ and $\rho=\sum_i p_i^2$. The ratio $\rho$ is a dimensionless concentration measure, invariant under multiplying all eigenvalues by a common positive constant. The reciprocal $1/\rho$ may be read as an effective spectral count, rather than an actual rank. When $M_2>0$, the scale-invariant derivative ratios are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratios}
 R_P:=\frac{P^{(4)}(0)}{P''(0)^2}=3(1-\rho),\qquad
 R_X:=\frac{X^{(4)}(0)}{X''(0)^2}=\frac{M_4M_0}{M_2^2}.
\end{equation}
A nonzero constant change of argument multiplies the numerator and denominator of either ratio by the same fourth power. Equality of the functions therefore requires $R_P=R_X$. Section~5 first quantifies the failure of this equality, then proves the obstruction without dividing by $M_2$.

\section{A certified Rayleigh bound}
\subsection{The interval test vector and triangular weight}
Let $f=2\mathbf1_{(0,1/4)}$, embedded as a real-valued vector in $H$. Its squared norm is four times the interval length, so $\norm{f}=1$. Its pairing with $A$ is real and equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T:=\pair{f}{Af}=4\int_0^{1/4}\!\int_0^{1/4}\Phi(x+y)\,dy\,dx.
\end{equation}
For $u=x+y$, allowable $x$ values lie between $\max(0,u-1/4)$ and $\min(1/4,u)$. Their interval length is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
 w(u)=\max\bigl(0,\min(u,1/2-u)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Thus $w(u)=u$ on $[0,1/4]$, $w(u)=1/2-u$ on $[1/4,1/2]$, and $w$ vanishes elsewhere. Fubini and translation of the restricted integrals give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
 T=4\int_0^{1/2}w(u)\Phi(u)\,du.
\end{equation}
The upper endpoint is $1/2$, not $1/4$, and the kernel remains in the integrand. Formally, the nonnegative integral identity is first proved in $\R_{\geq0}^{\infty}$. Finiteness permits conversion to real integrals; an explicit real-to-complex integral bridge identifies the complex pairing with~\eqref{eq:triangle}. Endpoints are immaterial for Lebesgue integration.

The rational certificate \code{hpThetaHankelTriangleRayleighLower_gt} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tbound}T>\frac{117}{500}.\end{equation}
External calculations may motivate the threshold but are not a premise. Positivity of this pairing follows from the certificate and does not assert positivity of $A$.

\subsection{From one test vector to the spectral square sum}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:Q}
For every $f\in H$ with $\norm f=1$, one has $\norm{Af}^4\leq Q$. In particular, the interval test vector yields $Q\geq T^4>(117/500)^4$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $(e_i)$ be the orthonormal spectral basis of $S$ and $\alpha_i=\pair{e_i}{f}$. The expansion $Sf=\sum_i\lambda_i\alpha_i e_i$ converges in $H$. The eigenvector equation gives $\pair{e_i}{Sf}=\lambda_i\alpha_i$. Since $\lambda_i^2\leq Q$, Parseval gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parseval}
 \norm{Sf}^2=\sum_i\lambda_i^2|\alpha_i|^2
 \leq Q\sum_i|\alpha_i|^2=Q\norm f^2.
\end{equation}
For $\norm f=1$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CS}
 \norm{Af}^2=\pair f{Sf}\leq\norm{Sf},\qquad\norm{Af}^4\leq Q.
\end{equation}
The pairing is real and nonnegative, so one may equivalently take its absolute value before applying Cauchy--Schwarz. For the interval test vector, $T=|\pair f{Af}|\leq\norm{Af}$ and $T>0$. Fourth powers prove the claim without selecting a largest eigenvalue.
\end{proof}
In exact form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qbound}
 Q>\left(\frac{117}{500}\right)^4
 =\frac{187388721}{62500000000}=0.002998219536.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The ratio interpretation and second-moment tolerance}
Put $a=27/1000$, $b=27/10000$, $m=12/25$, $e=17/200$, and $t=117/500$. Since $E>0$, the bounds on $Q$ and $E$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rhobound}
 \rho=\frac Q{E^2}>\frac{t^4}{e^2}
 =\frac{187388721}{451562500}>0.41497848.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for the ratios in~\eqref{eq:ratios},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratioseparation}
 R_P<\frac{792521337}{451562500}<1.755065
 <\frac{16}{9}<R_X,
 \qquad \frac{bm}{a^2}=\frac{16}{9}.
\end{equation}
Thus a hypothetical match needs a less concentrated spectrum than the test vector permits. Equivalently, the spectral effective count is less than $451562500/187388721\approx2.409763$.

The role of the $M_2$ upper bound can be stated precisely. Holding $b,m,e,t$ fixed, the denominator-free separation below is certified by any upper threshold $a$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tolerance}
 a^2<\frac{bm e^2}{3(e^2-t^4)}
 =\frac{195075}{264173779},\qquad
 a<0.0271741491\ldots.
\end{equation}
Here $e^2-t^4>0$. The supplied $a=0.027$ lies strictly below this threshold, with approximately $0.64\%$ relative headroom. This exposes the sensitivity of the proof to $M_2$ rather than hiding it in a coarse table. A smaller certified upper bound would enlarge the margin, but no stronger Lean certificate is part of the supplied record; we therefore retain $M_2<27/1000$ and make no claim that a new integral bound has been formalized.

\subsection{A denominator-free contradiction}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:strict}
The certified quantities satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict}
 3M_2^2(E^2-Q)<M_4M_0E^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $D=E^2-Q\geq0$. From $0\leq M_2<a$, obtain $M_2^2\leq a^2$ and $3M_2^2D\leq3a^2D$. Since $M_4>b$ and $M_0>m$, their product is at least $bm$. It suffices to show $3a^2(E^2-Q)<bmE^2$, or $(3a^2-bm)E^2<3a^2Q$. The coefficient $3a^2-bm=891/1000000$ is positive. Using $E<e$ and $Q>t^4$ reduces the claim to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin}
 3a^2t^4-(3a^2-bm)e^2
 =\frac{7476945327}{62500000000000000}>0.
\end{equation}
The margin is approximately $1.19631125232\times10^{-7}$, but exact arithmetic decides its sign. No division by $D$ or $M_2$ is needed, including when either vanishes.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:real}
For every real $c$, the functions $z\mapsto P(cz)$ and $X$ are unequal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $P(cz)=X(z)$ for all $z$. Differentiating twice and four times at zero gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matching}
 c^2E=\frac{M_2}{2M_0},\qquad
 c^4(E^2-Q)=\frac{M_4}{12M_0}.
\end{equation}
Since $E>0$ and $M_0>0$, eliminating $c$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant}
 3M_2^2(E^2-Q)=M_4M_0E^2,
\end{equation}
contradicting Proposition~\ref{prop:strict}. The case $c=0$ is included; no nonzero-scale assumption is needed.
\end{proof}
The coefficient $3$ is fixed by the derivative normalizations. Equation~\eqref{eq:invariant} is the decisive invariant. An overall mismatch in the second coefficient alone would be insufficient, since rescaling could correct it. The fourth-order comparison prevents that correction.

\section{Extension to every complex scale}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:real}
If $c\in\C$ and $P(cz)=X(z)$ for every $z$, then $\operatorname{Im}(c)=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The complex second-derivative identity, after clearing the positive denominator, reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complex}
 c^2(2EM_0)=M_2.
\end{equation}
The real quantities are embedded into $\C$. Since $2EM_0>0$ and $M_2\geq0$, the square $c^2$ is real and nonnegative. Write $c=x+iy$. Its imaginary part is $2xy$, so $xy=0$, while $x^2-y^2\geq0$. If $y\ne0$, then $x=0$ and $-y^2\geq0$, a contradiction. Thus $y=0$. If $M_2=0$, equation~\eqref{eq:complex} gives $c^2=0$, hence $c=0$ as well.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:complex}
There is no $c\in\C$ such that $P(cz)=X(z)$ for all $z\in\C$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exclusion}
 \neg\exists c\in\C,\quad(z\mapsto P(cz))=X.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:real} makes a hypothetical complex scale the real embedding of $\operatorname{Re}(c)$. The resulting real-scale identity contradicts Theorem~\ref{thm:real}.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}
For every $c\in\C$, there exists $z\in\C$ such that $P(cz)\ne X(z)$.
\end{corollary}
This is the pointwise existential form of function inequality, using classical logic. It neither selects an explicit mismatch point nor gives a numerical value for one.


\section{Moment-based Jensen polynomials}\label{sec:jensen}
\subsection{Coefficient normalization and the quadratic case}
The same kernel provides a sequence without introducing an operator spectrum:
\[
 \gamma(n)=\frac{n!}{(2n)!}M_{2n},
 \qquad
 J^{d,n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom dj\gamma(n+j)x^j.
\]
In the formal definitions, the even moment is a Bochner integral over $\operatorname{Ioi}(0)$. Defining such an integral is not itself a proof of integrability. The arguments used here establish the integrability required for moments of orders zero, two, and four. No all-orders integrability theorem is assumed in the quadratic proof.

The intended analytic expansion is
\[
 \Xi(iz)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma(n)}{n!}z^{2n}.
\]
This equality, including its convergence and interchange of integration with an infinite sum, is a subsequent formal proof obligation. It is not asserted by the Jensen foundations module. The low-order identities needed below are already available from the moment definitions and the derivative theory. Multiplying all coefficients by $1/M_0>0$ would normalize xi at zero without changing polynomial roots.

At degree two and shift zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jquadratic}
 \gamma(0)=M_0,\qquad \gamma(1)=\frac{M_2}{2},\qquad
 \gamma(2)=\frac{M_4}{12},\qquad
 J^{2,0}(x)=M_0+M_2x+\frac{M_4}{12}x^2.
\end{equation}
Since the kernel is positive on the nonnegative half-line and the weighted integrals are finite, $M_0>0$ and $M_4>0$. Thus this polynomial has degree exactly two. Its discriminant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discriminant}
 D=M_2^2-\frac{M_0M_4}{3}
   =4\gamma(1)^2-4\gamma(0)\gamma(2).
\end{equation}
Consequently its hyperbolicity is equivalent to $M_0M_4\le3M_2^2$; strict inequality gives distinct roots. This is an elementary equivalence for this polynomial, not an equivalence between a single moment inequality and RH.

\subsection{Certified strict hyperbolicity}
\begin{theorem}[Certified moment gap]\label{thm:jgap}
The moments of the differential theta kernel satisfy
\[
 M_0\le\frac{501}{1000},\qquad
 M_2\ge\frac{227}{10000},\qquad
 M_4\le\frac{3}{1000}.
\]
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jgap}
 3M_2^2-M_0M_4\ge\frac{4287}{100000000}>0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The integral certificates proving the three bounds are described in Section~\ref{sec:jcert}. Positivity permits multiplication of the upper bounds for $M_0$ and $M_4$; the positive lower bound for $M_2$ permits squaring. Exact rational arithmetic gives
\[
 3\left(\frac{227}{10000}\right)^2
  -\frac{501}{1000}\frac{3}{1000}
  =\frac{4287}{100000000}.
\]
The final Lean proof combines these certified inequalities with polynomial arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic factorization and complete root classification]\label{thm:jroots}
Put $a=M_4/12$ and
\[
 r_\pm=\frac{-M_2\pm\sqrt D}{2a}.
\]
Then $r_-<r_+$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jfactor}
 J^{2,0}(x)=a(x-r_+)(x-r_-).
\end{equation}
The complexified polynomial has precisely the roots $r_+$ and $r_-$, viewed in $\C$. Every complex root has imaginary part zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:jgap} gives $D>0$; in fact the mathematical bound $D\ge1429/10^8$ follows by division by three. Also $a>0$. The identity $(\sqrt D)^2=D$ yields
$r_++r_-=-M_2/a$ and $r_+r_-=M_0/a$.
Expanding the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:jfactor} proves the polynomial identity. Since $\sqrt D>0$ and $2a>0$, the roots are distinct and ordered. Applying the coefficient embedding $\R\hookrightarrow\C$ preserves the factorization. The absence of zero divisors in $\C$, together with $a\ne0$, gives the two-root classification and hence realness of every root.
\end{proof}
In Lean the roots are first expressed using $\gamma(1)$ and $\gamma(2)$; equation~\eqref{eq:jquadratic} identifies these expressions with $r_\pm$ above. The complex theorem is about the mapped polynomial, not about the zeros of $\Xi$.

\subsection{Relation to the classical Jensen criterion}
Farmer's critique~\cite{farmer} concerns the usefulness of Jensen methods for attacking RH, not the failure of the classical logical equivalence. Hyperbolicity of the full appropriate xi Jensen family, under the criterion's analytic hypotheses, remains equivalent to RH. Large-shift hyperbolicity and Hermite-type limits can instead occur broadly and do not settle the missing cases. We formalize $J^{2,0}$ as a target in its own right: a concrete theorem with certified integration, an explicit margin, and a complete root classification. Its value here is the reusable formal proof infrastructure, rather than evidence for RH.

The Tur\'an inequalities for the xi coefficient sequence have a substantial classical literature, including Csordas, Norfolk, and Varga~\cite{cnv}. Our contribution is the formal certificate and root classification, rather than a claim that the degree-two inequality is new. Griffin, Ono, Rolen, and Zagier~\cite{gorz} discuss the Jensen criterion for RH and establish hyperbolicity in large-shift regimes for each fixed degree.

The full classical criterion requires an entire family of Jensen polynomials of the appropriate xi coefficient sequence. Our formalization covers $(d,n)=(2,0)$ only; the general classical Jensen criterion discussed in~\cite{cnv,gorz} is not formalized in this development. A formal use of that criterion would additionally require the all-orders coefficient bridge, the hypotheses of the relevant entire-function criterion, and a proof covering the quantified polynomial family. The existing finite certificate supplies none of those universal conclusions.

There is no tension between the two principal results. The quadratic theorem gives
$M_0M_4/M_2^2<3$, while the spectral obstruction places the product's fourth-order ratio strictly below this same target ratio. Hyperbolicity of this quadratic is compatible with failure of the proposed spectral-product identity.

\section{Finite integration certificates for the Jensen bound}\label{sec:jcert}
\subsection{Rational cells and kernel envelopes}
The new moment certificates partition $[0,1]$ into 1600 cells,
\[
 \ell_i=\frac{i}{1600},\qquad
 u_i=\frac{i+1}{1600},\qquad 0\le i<1600.
\]
The checked files group twenty cells into each of eighty batches. These batches are distinct from the older 400-endpoint fourth-moment lower certificate used for the spectral obstruction.

On each open cell, rational endpoint constructions give lower and upper bounds $L_i\le\Phi(u)\le U_i$ with nonnegative envelopes. For $m\in\{0,2,4\}$, monotonicity of the nonnegative power gives
\[
 \ell_i^m L_i\le u^m\Phi(u)\le u_i^m U_i.
\]
Here the zeroth power equals one, including at zero. Integration multiplies the constant bounds by the cell length $1/1600$. This step uses integrability theorems and integral monotonicity, rather than interpreting pointwise samples as quadrature estimates.

The endpoint arithmetic uses the rational enclosure
\[
 \frac{3141592}{10^6}\le\pi\le\frac{3141593}{10^6}.
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Exponential certificates use a finite Taylor sum and a controlled remainder. For $0\le x\le1$, writing
$T_{12}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{11}x^j/j!$, the construction uses
\[
 T_{12}(x)\le e^x\le
 T_{12}(x)+\frac{13x^{12}}{12\cdot12!}.
\]
The upper remainder follows by bounding the successive tail-term ratios by $1/13$. Scaling by 32 and using $e^y=(e^{y/32})^{32}$ allows the endpoint values to be enclosed by rational arithmetic in the range required by the certificates. The kernel envelopes also account for the theta-series tail; retaining finitely many theta terms without a remainder would not establish an upper bound.

It connects integrals over open cells to interval integrals and telescopes along the rational partition. Endpoints contribute zero measure, so the use of open rather than half-open cells does not lose integral mass.

The second-moment tail is nonnegative, so a numerical upper bound for it is unnecessary for the lower bound on $M_2$. \section{Scope of the exclusion}
The theorem excludes equality with the fixed normalized target after a constant complex change of argument. It includes real, imaginary, zero, and general complex scales. The normalization $X(0)=P(0)=1$ is part of the statement; the identity is not merely proportionality at unspecified normalization.

The conclusion does not assert different zero sets. A nowhere-vanishing entire factor may change Taylor coefficients while preserving zeros and multiplicities; multiplication by $\exp(az^2+bz^4)$ is an example. More precisely, if for a fixed $c$ the nonzero entire functions $P(c\,\cdot)$ and $X$ had identical zeros with identical multiplicities, their quotient would extend to a nowhere-vanishing entire function on $\C$. It would admit an entire logarithm, yielding $P(cz)=X(z)\exp(g(z))$ for an entire $g$. This is conditional, not an asserted zero correspondence. Zero-set equality without multiplicity information does not justify the quotient argument.

Positivity of $S=A^*A$ does not establish a spectral realization of xi zeros. Such a realization requires a separate correspondence theorem, including completeness and multiplicity. The present paper proves that the direct product identity~\eqref{eq:exclusion} cannot supply that correspondence for this construction; it gives no contradiction to the Riemann hypothesis.

The work claims no priority over all possible informal obstructions to this kernel. Its verifiable contribution is the explicit quantitative obstruction and formal dependency chain. Broader novelty comparisons require a dedicated review of theta-kernel spectral models beyond the contextual references used here.

\section{Future research}
\noindent\textbf{Eigenvalue decay and zero counting.} A complementary obstruction could compare quantitative decay of the eigenvalues with the growth and zero-counting properties required by xi. Since a positive eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ contributes zeros at $z=\pm\lambda_i^{-1/2}$, a bound on the eigenvalue counting function near zero would control the zero count of the product. The rapid decay of the kernel suggests studying finite-rank approximation errors and the resulting singular-value bounds, then translating them to eigenvalue decay for $S=A^*A$. Any exclusion derived this way would require proved decay estimates, justified counting asymptotics, and a formal bridge from spectral data to product zeros. Kernel decay alone is not such a proof. This argument remains future work and is not used by the current audit.

\noindent\textbf{Sharper moment certificates.} A smaller rational upper threshold for $M_2$, certified in Lean and propagated to the arithmetic theorem, would enlarge the separation in~\eqref{eq:margin}. Equation~\eqref{eq:tolerance} identifies the exact admissible threshold for the present companion bounds. Refinement should retain the same kernel normalization and provide the certificate source and audit output.

A new candidate should be tested against second and fourth coefficient identities before undertaking a large formal development. Definitions of moments, trace quantities, and normalized target must be explicit; numerical exploration can guide thresholds but cannot replace analytic proofs or kernel-checked certificates.

An identity allowing an additional entire factor is a different problem. Admissible factors need explicit normalization and growth constraints; unrestricted factors can conceal the identification problem. A nonlinear change of argument likewise requires a separate analysis of its effect on zeros and multiplicities. Neither modification is established here.

Any bridge to the Riemann hypothesis must identify the relevant spectral data with xi zeros, including completeness and multiplicity. The current result supplies an exclusion test for one direct product identity. Completing the immutable release metadata and recording an independent clean audit build are necessary reproducibility steps before submission.

\section{Conclusion}
The development provides two complementary results from the same differential theta kernel. A Rayleigh estimate and certified fourth-order separation exclude equality of the adjoint-square spectral product with normalized xi after every constant complex rescaling. A separate 1600-cell integral certificate proves a strict quadratic Jensen inequality with an explicit rational margin, followed by factorization and complete complex-root classification. The latter is a formalization of a particular classical hyperbolicity statement, not a universal Jensen theorem or a proof of RH. The supplied audits report the standard axiom list without \code{sorryAx}; a pinned combined release and independent clean rebuild remain necessary for full reproducibility.
